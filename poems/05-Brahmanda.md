All that I have found beautiful in my days:

—the peachfingered dawn, the naranjitas kissing the morning table, their oil on
my fingers, on the rim of my full cup, the cool of his belly, its tattoo of thyme, its
delicate fat, the herbs in the oil, the oil pooled on the bread, the children
screaming the pigeons into flight in the square heart of the city, the cities
themselves, shivering in the night like tamed stars, the stars themselves, winking
through the distance, the scent of seawet hair in the staggering night—

all that I have found beautiful in my days I have eaten.
Until nothing was left but the end of the world.
At the end of the world:

—an egg.

This, too, I ate.
