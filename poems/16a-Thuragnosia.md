In a way, the diagnosis was an honor,
the doctor assured. A new flaw of the body

dashed through the brain as a seam of cobalt
ore to be mined for perspective and citation.

Of course, there were difficulties. Getting in,
for one. Getting out, for another. But

this was not so different than the life before.
We all need help. People palm the world open

for us, unthinking. He came to appreciate this.
Still, of course, changes had to be made.

Whitman would be pleased. The locks, the jambs,
the doors: all now removed from his perception

by neuronal quirk, as well as from his home
by tipped professionals. And so he resided

comfortably in a house of arches and gaps.
Thus established, he wanted more. Access

to theaters, bakeries, his old cafes. Doctor
dearest suggested a solution old and elegant:

knocking.

Thus, when there was no one to open a door,
he would circle the walls with percussive hands,

making rhythms on cool stone, warm wood,
the breastbones of friends—until admission.

He came to love the sound.
